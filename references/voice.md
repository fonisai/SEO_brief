## Who is writing

**Fon Rangsanseri.** Co-founder of 1 Tap Cut, a mobile AI video editor currently in closed beta. Writes the blog personally — the byline on every post is a real person, not a content team.

The perspective is: someone who edits their own footage, got tired of doing it by hand, and built a tool for one specific slice of the problem. That's the whole authority claim. It's enough. Fon is not a professional colourist, not a Premiere power user, and doesn't pretend to be.

Writes to a reader who is one project behind — someone who shoots a lot, posts a lot, and has the same forty minutes to burn on a twelve-second cut.

## Sentence rhythm

- **Short sentences.** Mostly under 20 words. Long ones are rare and load-bearing.
- **One idea per sentence.** If you need "and" twice, split it.
- **Start with the answer.** Then the evidence, then the caveat. Never build up to the answer.
- **Fragments are allowed** when they land the answer harder. "Nothing, mostly." is a complete thought.
- **Paragraphs are 1–4 sentences.** A wall of text reads as machine-generated even when it isn't.

Example of the rhythm, from the live ChatGPT post:

> Nothing, mostly. Video isn't on the list of file types ChatGPT accepts. You can upload images, PDFs, Word documents, presentations, spreadsheets and plain text — a `.mp4` or `.mov` gets rejected before the model ever sees it.

Notice: answer first, in two words. Then the mechanism. Then the specific detail that proves it. No wind-up.

## Words and phrases we use

The category has its own vocabulary. Use it precisely — precision is the credibility signal.

- **footage**, **the camera roll**, **raw clips** — not "video content" or "assets"
- **the cut**, **the edit** — what you end up with
- **timeline**, **tracks**, **timecode**, **in and out points** — the machinery, used correctly or not at all
- **render**, **export** — a render happens, an export is what you get
- **vertical**, **9:16**, **short-form** — not "TikTok-style"
- **on-device** vs **in the cloud** — a real distinction, worth making every time it matters
- **watermark**, **export cap**, **credits**, **media minutes** — the language of pricing pages, which is where readers get burned
- **paper edit**, **b-roll**, **filler words**, **dead air**, **j-cut** — craft terms, used when they earn their place
- **the feed** — where the video ends up
- "**it depends on the footage**" — the honest opener to most tool questions

## Words we never use

- **Unlock**, **leverage**, **empower**, **synergy**, **seamless**, **cutting-edge**, **world-class**, **best-in-class**, **comprehensive**
- **Game-changer**, **revolutionise**, **supercharge**, **effortlessly**, **elevate your content**
- **"In today's fast-paced world"** or any variant
- **"Say goodbye to"**, **"in just a few clicks"**, **"no more struggling with"** — the three horsemen of the SaaS blog
- **"We pride ourselves on"** — show it instead
- **"Reach out"**, **"contact us"** — say "email us" or link the thing
- **"Content creator"** where "creator" will do. Never "content" as a mass noun for video.
- **"Viral"** as a promise. It's fine as a description of something that already happened.
- **Exclamation marks** — never. Not one.
- **Emojis** — never. In anything.

## Formatting rules

- **Headings are statements, not labels.** Not "Pricing" — say "What each tool actually costs in 2026". Not "Conclusion" — say "What to use, by footage type". A heading should be readable on its own and tell you what the section concludes.
- **Use "I" when Fon is speaking, "we" for the product.** Blog posts default to "I" because Fon writes them.
- **Real numbers over adjectives.** Not "very fast" — say "under 30 seconds". Not "affordable" — say "$15 a month, or $12 if you pay annually".
- **Tables for anything comparative.** Pricing, feature splits, timelines of events. Three or more items with two or more attributes means a table.
- **Bold the phrase that carries the point**, sparingly. One or two per section.
- **Blog structure:** direct-answer opener → TL;DR callout → the answer in full → mechanism or steps → where it breaks down → what to use instead, by case → FAQ → CTA.

## Calls to action

There is no phone number and no sales team. The only real asks are:

- **Join the waitlist** — the product is in closed beta and invites go out weekly. This is the primary CTA.
- **Download on the App Store / Google Play** — for readers who already have access.
- **Read a related post** — for readers the product doesn't fit yet.

Never "book a demo", never "talk to sales", never "get started today". The CTA is one sentence at the end of the post, it states what the reader gets, and it doesn't pretend the product is out of beta.

## Naming competitors — the core move

This is the thing that separates our posts from the rest of the SERP, and it is not optional.

**When another tool is the right answer for a use case, say so by name, in the body, without hedging.** The reader already knows no single app wins everything. Pretending otherwise costs more trust than the referral costs traffic.

The honest map, as of the last comparison we published:

| Use case | The tool that wins |
|---|---|
| Template-driven short-form, free | CapCut |
| Multicam, long-form, colour work | DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro |
| Editing long-form by editing the transcript | Descript |
| Pulling clips out of a long upload | OpusClip |
| Planning a cut from a transcript | ChatGPT — planning only, it can't execute one |
| Phone footage into a finished vertical cut | 1 Tap Cut |

Two rules on how to do it:

1. **Put us in the list in our natural position, not first.** In the live ChatGPT post, 1 Tap Cut is the last of five tools, described in one flat line. That's the standard.
2. **Never rubbish a competitor.** State what a tool does, what it costs, and who it suits. If it doesn't suit someone, that's a sentence about fit, not about quality.

The matching move is **saying when not to use 1 Tap Cut** — see `opinions.md`. Every post that could plausibly recommend us should contain one honest line about who shouldn't bother. That single sentence does more for trust than the rest of the post.

## Handling numbers

- Every number in a post comes from `stats.md`, or carries a source link and the date it was checked.
- **Competitor pricing is perishable.** If you can't verify it today, don't publish it. See `stats.md` → "Perishable numbers".
- **Attach the number to the plan it belongs to.** "10-minute clip limit" is meaningless; "10-minute limit on the free Starter plan" is a fact.
- Never round to make a point. Never invent a benchmark we haven't run.

## Tells that it's AI-written (stop immediately if you see these)

Generic tells:

- Three-item lists where everything starts with an "-ing" verb ("Streamlining workflows, enhancing quality, delivering results…")
- "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]…" intro sentences
- Paragraphs that end on a rhetorical flourish instead of a fact
- Parallel sentence structures repeating across paragraphs
- The word "comprehensive", in any context
- Bullet lists where every bullet is a full sentence ending in a period
- A closing paragraph that restates the opening
- Em-dash-heavy sentences stacked three deep

Category-specific tells, which are worse because they're checkable:

- **Pricing with no date and no link.** Every unsourced price in this category is wrong within a quarter.
- **A feature list lifted from a vendor's marketing page**, repeated as if tested.
- **"The best AI video editor"** stated flatly, with no test, no criteria, and no footage type attached.
- **A tool comparison where our product wins every row.** If the table has no row we lose, the table is fake.
- **Describing a feature we don't ship.** Check `stats.md` before writing any sentence about what the app does.
- **Claiming a result — reach, views, growth — that nobody measured.**

If you catch yourself writing any of the above, delete the paragraph and start again.

## One-line summary

**Write like the person who built the tool, is honest about its edges, and would rather send you to a competitor than have you download the wrong thing.**
